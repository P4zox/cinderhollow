Cinderhollow Room Expansion

Every region grows from a handful of rooms into a place worth exploring, with the kinds of room that suit it. The library gets puzzles, the storm-swept spire gets parkour, the foundry gets fights, the observatory gets views of the sky. No existing room is removed and saves keep working.

The room kit

Eight kinds of room, each dressed in its region's own tiles, enemies and hazards. A region only gets the kinds that make sense for it; the grid below shows the mix. One screen is about 24×14 tiles. Most rooms today are 48×14, two screens wide.

Parkour follows your abilities

Parkour on the main path only needs what you're guaranteed to have when you arrive. Trials and secrets can need later abilities, so they become reasons to come back. The map marks them with a lock until you have what they need.

The Last Field

A secret vista in the Stormward Spire, found only after Cindervane is dead and the storm has had time to clear.

How it appears

Kill Cindervane, then leave the Stormspire. The next time you come back, after resting at any shrine or at least five minutes after the fight, the wall where she fell has split open. Warm wind and wheat seeds blow through the crack, and a soft chime plays when you enter the room. Nothing on the map shows it until you walk in.

The field

A wide sunset scene: a low sun, long clouds, far hills in several parallax layers and a golden wheat field that sways and parts around you as you walk. Embers and seeds drift in the light. There are no enemies. The music is a slow, warm variation of the Spire theme. At the far end is her nest: a hollow in the wheat ringed with shed scales.

Her story

A lore stone and the nest tell it. The Hallow was once fields like this, and the drakes kept the sky over them. When the Pale Root rose, the sky went grey and the drakes died out one by one. Cindervane was the last, and she guarded the one warm place left. The boss’s own line, “The sky was ours before the Root. It will be ours after,” turns out to be about what she left in the nest.

The reward: Ember Hatchling

The nest holds her last egg, still warm. Taking it teaches a new spell. Ember Hatchling summons a tiny drake cub that fights at your side for 10 seconds, then scatters into embers.
• It flies beside you and spits small fireballs at the nearest enemy.
• Now and then it dives in for a biting attack that sets the target on fire.
• Damage scales with Faith. Only one cub at a time.
• It can’t be hit, and it never makes a fight impossible to lose.

What gets made

• The cub sprite: appear, fly, spit, dive and vanish.
• The spell icon.
• Five parallax layers for the field, plus animated wheat.
• The nest and egg props, and the lore stone.
• The music variation.
• The new-spell toast and a Chronicle page.

Built when

Phase 1, with the Spire. It uses the vista bench and hidden-passage pieces from Phase 0, so it arrives with everything else in that region.

What gets built first

These engine pieces are shared by every region, so they come before any rooms.

Doors and passages

Rooms today only connect when they touch edge to edge on one world grid, and the middle of the map is full. The NEO-HALLOW portal becomes a general door: step through a doorway, a crack or a hatch, and you arrive at its partner. New rooms can then sit in open map space and still show up next to their region on the map.

Trial framework

A sigil at the start of the trial. Touching a hazard puts you back at the start instantly, with no HP lost and no walk back. A timer runs, with a par time for a bonus. The first clear opens the reward chest. Best times are saved and shown on the map.

Moving parts

New reusable pieces, re-skinned per region:
• Moving platforms on a set path.
• Crumbling ledges.
• Timed phase platforms that blink on a beat.
• Sinking stones.
• Swinging blades.
• Rising hazard floors for chase rooms.

Puzzle parts

Levers and switches wired to several gates. Timed gates. Pressure plates. Ordered sequences (bells, lanterns, stops) with clues written into the room. Beams you can redirect (Starfall lenses, Dunes mirrors). Water or slag levels that raise and lower.

Optional gauntlets

Each gauntlet has a challenge marker themed on its region. The room stays open until you use the marker. Then the gates lock, a wave counter appears and the fight begins. Once cleared, the marker stays lit, the gates stay open and the fight can be replayed. A gauntlet never blocks the main path.

Vista benches

Sit on a bench and the camera pulls back to show the whole scene. The music drops to the region's ambient layer, and a lore stone or ghost can speak. There are no enemies, and resting there is safe. Every bench found is logged in a Vistas list.

Bigger rooms

Grand rooms of 100–130 tiles wide or 40–60 tall, with two or three routes through them. The camera, room cache and memory budget get checked at that size, and the room layer is drawn in chunks if needed.

Map upgrade

With twice as many rooms, the map gets zoom and pan, region filters, and icons for trials, puzzles, vistas, secrets and locked rooms. Cleared trials get a gold mark.

Reachability check

A build-time tool that uses the real jump height, dash and wall-jump distances to check that every exit and reward can be reached with the abilities a room claims to need. Impossible parkour fails the build. Headless test runs then do the rest.

Rewards worth the detour

Trial charms

Each of the 13 trials guards a new charm themed on its region. Many change how you move or fight: a longer air dash, a wall-cling that lasts longer, a third pogo off spikes, a faster glide.

Upgrade materials

Gauntlets and puzzles pay out shards, seeds and flask upgrades, so side content makes you stronger without being required.

Lore pages

Vistas and secrets hold pages of the Hallow Chronicle, a new Lore tab in the inventory. A complete set unlocks a final vista with an extra story scene.

Completion

The Status tab tracks rooms, trials, vistas and secrets, per region and overall.

Delivery

Each phase is published when it's done, so your friends play the new rooms early instead of waiting for all 151.

Decisions for you

Trial failure. Touching a hazard in a trial either resets you to the start instantly with no damage, or hurts you like normal play.
Recommended: instant reset, no damage. Retrying is quick, and a trial can be hard without being tedious.
How hard trials get. Hollow Knight's White Palace level, or a step below it.
Recommended: story-region trials a step below; the side-region trials and the Ember's final trial at full White Palace difficulty.
The mix. Some regions now have no trial (Ramparts, Catacombs, Mire, Crimson Manor), no vista (Catacombs, the Deep, Necropolis) or no puzzle (Spire, Crown, Thornveil).
Recommended: keep these gaps. Regions feel different when they aren't all built from the same checklist.
Where new rooms connect. Doors let rooms sit anywhere, but walking straight through an edge feels more seamless.
Recommended: edge connections wherever the map has space (most region borders do), and doors only for optional rooms in packed areas.
Order. Story regions first, or the side regions your friends are exploring now.
Recommended: story regions first, as planned above.
